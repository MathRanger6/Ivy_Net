Charles identifies the Army HERO as the observation that motivated this research program. His work on Army officers’ performance, peer environments, promotion and attrition produced outcome displays whose rise and upper-end downturn prompted a question that individual performance alone did not answer: how might an individual’s position among peers relate to their prospects for advancement?

The scientific interest was not simply that stronger performers tend to succeed. It was that advancement might depend on the environment in which a person performs—and that the relationship might not keep increasing.

There is an important historical distinction. A reviewed Army example used **own rating minus the leave-one-out peer average**, a measure of relative standing. That is not the peer average alone. ## 2. Why a HERO plot as well as a naïve plot?

“Naïve plot” is Charles’s longstanding name for **own measured performance versus observed advancement rate**. It predates the deterministic benchmark model. It asks: are stronger performers more likely to advance? In basketball, the current canonical plot shows draft rates rising across performance bins.

A **HERO plot** is the project’s name for an empirical outcome display organized around peer context or relative standing. It asks a different question: how does advancement vary across environments or positions within them? The exact horizontal measure must be stated. HERO is not one estimator, a causal effect, or a guarantee of an inverted-U shape.

A useful extension compares people in narrower own-performance bands across the peer-environment axis. The motivating possibility is that equally capable people may have different prospects in different settings, particularly near a competitive selection margin. A broad band does not literally hold performance fixed, and “band of excellence” is a working label, not a universal percentile.

The HERO therefore supplements the naïve plot. A strong own-performance association does not establish that group context is irrelevant. Conversely, an environmental association may reflect sorting, differences in performance, institutions or measurement rather than an independent peer effect. Explaining why this is a scientifically worthwhile question is the introduction’s job; proving a preferred mechanism is not its starting assumption.

## 3. Domains explored and what they contribute

### Army: promotion, performance and peer context

This is the originating domain, involving officer evaluations, peer pools, promotion from Captain to Major, and departure. Senior-rater pools are not automatically equivalent to organizational units. Time-to-promotion, competing departure and unresolved follow-up complicate what “success probability” means. Existing exported analyses and plots provide substantive motivation; their axes, timing and estimators need reconciliation before final empirical claims. Survival and competing-risks work supports the broader question of how advancement should be measured.

### NCAA men’s basketball: performance, teammates and the draft

This is the most actively developed empirical comparison and model-development setting. Work includes own-performance plots, teammate-environment HERO plots, performance-band comparisons, shared and leave-one-out measures, and sensitivity to filters and binning. Performance measures explored include points per minute (PPM) and box plus/minus (BPM); PPM is the current Jazz Night baseline.

Some historical high-performance-band displays show an environmental downturn; other specifications do not. For example, a recent BPM roster-pressure comparison did not show the anticipated broad high-pressure downturn. The record must retain both. Very low playing time, repeated player-seasons versus final-season records, and changing band definitions have mattered. The outcome in these comparisons is typically ever drafted, not selection from a verified annual risk set.

### Academic careers: publication performance, departments and tenure

The project has built tenure HERO displays using publication-based performance and departmental peer measures. A documented decision-year display uses the leave-one-out mean career publication rate of other faculty in the department. Its 389 included people have resolved outcomes: 191 tenured and 198 classified as attrited. The saved binned curve has an interior peak and a lower upper-end rate.

That is descriptive evidence among selected resolved cases, not automatically tenure probability by a common career year for all entrants. Censored and transferred cases are excluded. Its substantive value is a further setting in which individual success and the surrounding group can be examined; it is not yet proof of a shared cross-domain mechanism.

### Romanian education: prior peers and selective educational transitions

Work has recovered and linked historical 2001 admissions records to examine applicants’ prior-school environments, performance and subsequent placements. A saved 22-county descriptive pilot extends beyond initial data acquisition. Outcomes depend on how selective programs are defined; linkage, available admission rounds and county coverage constrain interpretation. The records do not represent every eighth grader or a completed national causal study.

This is an additional domain under investigation, not a requirement that the introduction promise a fourth fully validated application.

## 4. What has been built—and what remains open

The repository contains empirical pipelines and figures, a substantial working model chapter, simulations, historical fitting work, and methodological investigations. It is not an empty proposal.

The advancement model distinguishes **scoring** from **selection**. It starts with individual performance, allows uncertain selection, and explores whether a congestion term can account for environmental patterns. Full-group mean performance, leave-one-out peer mean and roster pressure are distinct candidates; a single cross-domain winner has not been established. Developmental benefits of peers belong in the introduction’s wider motivation, although the current competitive model holds input performance fixed.

The current Jazz Night progression has locked an empirical PPM plot and a pooled deterministic top-K comparison. A corrected stochastic comparison is ready for review; no new temperature fit has been completed. Historical fits are not automatically fits of the current exact-K selection rule.

The **Levine–Gates (LG) group-formation model is a small supporting piece**, per Charles’s latest clarification. It should not organize the introduction or be promoted into a major standalone chapter by this agent.

The dissertation’s identity is network science, connecting group structure and peer environments with the science of individual success. Domains supply evidence and contrasts. We have not established that all domains have the same curve, causal mechanism or outcome probability.
