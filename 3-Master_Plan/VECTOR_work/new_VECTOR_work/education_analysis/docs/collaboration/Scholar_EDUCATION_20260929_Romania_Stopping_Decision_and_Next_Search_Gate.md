## 1. Decision

Romania should be preserved as a **supporting empirical and theoretical case**, not pursued now as a fourth full replication.

We are stopping because of **institutional fit**, before searching transformations or running outcome analyses to obtain a preferred curve.

This is not a failed analysis. The Romania work established that the setting is scientifically useful, but it lacks a clearly observed **scarce downstream selection event** analogous to Army promotion/top-block rationing or NBA draft selection.

The proposed Romania outcome analysis is therefore paused.

### Reopen Romania only if

1. a documented downstream scarce selection outcome linked to these students becomes available; or
2. a specific dissertation need later justifies examining environmental development/relative-position effects themselves.

---

## 2. What Romania contributes

Romania provides an unusually clean sequence:

**prior measured performance → capacity-constrained assignment → identifiable school/track peer environment → later academic performance**

The published paper also provides quantitative survey evidence that access to stronger environments can coexist with adverse behavioral responses.

Students marginally gaining access to stronger schools:

- encounter stronger peers;
- perceive themselves as relatively weaker within their peer environment;
- report somewhat more negative peer interactions;
- experience changes in parental behavior;
- enter a stratified system in which teachers themselves sort.

At the same time, access to the stronger school is associated with modestly better later Baccalaureate performance.

This makes Romania useful for a broad conceptual decomposition:

\[
L=B-D
\]

where:

- \(L\) = net observed learning/development outcome;
- \(B\) = benefits associated with the stronger environment;
- \(D\) = drawbacks or adverse responses associated with that environment.

Romania is consistent with the possibility that both \(B>0\) and \(D>0\) while the net academic effect remains positive.

However, the data do **not** separately identify the magnitudes of \(B\) and \(D\), and the adverse responses cannot be assigned a causal performance penalty from the evidence currently available.

---

## 3. Important conceptual boundary: drawback is not automatically congestion

Relative-position pressure, feelings of marginalization, reduced parental help, and other adverse responses are scientifically relevant, but they should not automatically be labeled **congestion**.

For congestion in the dissertation's stronger sense, we should be able to identify a scarce resource, distinction, or opportunity for which individuals compete.

Examples might include:

- limited top evaluations;
- promotion slots;
- draft selections;
- scholarships;
- honors;
- selective program seats;
- scarce course seats;
- graduate-school admission;
- another rationed opportunity.

Romania clearly contains scarcity in **entry into the school/track environment**.

The later Baccalaureate outcome, however, is not a fixed-\(K\) downstream distinction awarded among the students after they have spent time in that environment.

Therefore Romania supports the existence of **benefits plus adverse relative-position mechanisms**, but does not by itself establish the full congestion-and-selection mechanism.

---

## 4. Why we are not constructing another algebraic outcome

No currently proposed transformation solves the missing institutional problem.

Residualized Baccalaureate grades, percentile ranks, own-minus-peer scores, gain-like measures, or artificial "top scorer" indicators may be useful descriptions, but none creates an observed scarce downstream selection event.

We will not:

- search transformations until an inverted-U appears;
- manufacture a top-\(K\) Baccalaureate outcome;
- redefine success after inspecting curves;
- interpret a residualized outcome as a newly observed causal mechanism.

The absence of another algebraic outcome is not why Romania is being paused. The reason is that its institutional structure answers only part of the dissertation's central question.

## 5. What the next dataset must contain

The next search is not for another generic education peer-effects dataset.

The target sequence is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{prior performance}
\rightarrow
\text{identifiable peer environment}
\rightarrow
\text{peer composition}
\rightarrow
\text{actual scarce downstream opportunity}
\rightarrow
\text{observed selection}
}
\]

### Binding refinements from new VECTOR

**Prior performance should precede entry into the peer environment wherever possible.**  
A score measured after entry may already incorporate the environment we are trying to study.

**The peer pool and competitive pool may differ.**  
Classmates may shape development, information, visibility, and relative position while students later compete at a school, regional, state, or national level for a scarce opportunity.

Both pools must be identified. They do not need to coincide.

**Eligible, applicant, offer, and accepted populations must remain distinct.**  
A defensible selection fraction requires specifying which competition is being measured.

Where possible distinguish:

- \(N_{eligible}\)
- \(N_{applicant}\)
- \(K_{offer}\)
- \(K_{accepted/enrolled}\)

Do not use a single \(K/N\) label when the institutional risk set is ambiguous.

## 6. Candidate downstream selection events

Search specifically for settings with an observed limited opportunity **after meaningful exposure to the peer environment**, such as:

- competitive scholarships or fellowships;
- rank-based academic awards or honors;
- selective majors/programs entered after college enrollment;
- capped advanced tracks entered after time in a school;
- selective internships or placements;
- limited research opportunities;
- honors-college admission after enrollment;
- graduate/professional-school admission;
- centralized course/program allocation with binding capacity;
- other institutionally rationed distinctions.

The downstream event must be real, not constructed solely for our analysis.

---

## 7. Search evaluation grid

For every serious candidate, separately evaluate:

1. pre-environment prior performance;
2. identifiable peer environment;
3. time spent/exposure in that environment;
4. peer composition measurable from pre-environment performance;
5. realized sorting;
6. actual downstream scarce opportunity;
7. competitive pool definition;
8. eligible population;
9. applicant population;
10. number of offers/awards \(K\);
11. acceptance/enrollment if distinct;
12. observed winners/non-winners;
13. longitudinal linkage from prior performance through selection;
14. public/replication/application/restricted access;
15. credible identification opportunity;
16. whether the peer pool and competitive pool are the same or different.

A candidate with rich achievement outcomes but no observed scarce selection should not be promoted merely because it is easy to download.

## 8. Early lead discovered during the narrower search

A particularly important literature lead is:

**Mark R. Rosenzweig and Bing Xu, "Class Peers as Competitors and Educators: The Consequences of Rank-Based Academic Rewards" (2026 revision of NBER Working Paper 31135).**

This paper is theoretically close to the dissertation mechanism. It explicitly treats classmates as both:

- **educators**, through peer learning and assistance; and
- **competitors**, because rewards depend on relative academic standing.

The paper reports that in schools emphasizing rank-based rewards, stronger peers reduce effort and peer assistance among high-performing incumbent students, while weaker students are less affected.

This is unusually close to the \(B-D\) logic and to the idea that congestion/competition is most consequential within a band of students who plausibly compete for the scarce distinction.

However, the paper uses **restricted-use NELS:88** plus external administrative information on refugee assignments. Therefore it is presently more valuable as:

1. a high-priority theoretical/mechanism paper;
2. a clue that relevant competition-policy variables may exist in the NELS family;
3. a guide for what variables to seek in public datasets;

than as an immediately confirmed public replication dataset.

This lead should be investigated carefully before initiating restricted-use acquisition.

---

## 9. Another candidate class: course allocation

Public replication data exist for Budish & Cantillon's Harvard Business School course-allocation study. The mechanism has genuinely scarce course seats and observed student preferences/allocation.

This is useful because it cleanly distinguishes:

- applicants/preferences;
- limited course capacity;
- allocation outcomes.

But it may fail our longitudinal requirement if the available data do not provide strong **pre-environment ability**, a meaningful preceding peer environment, and later individual success.

Therefore course-allocation datasets should not be promoted solely because they have excellent \(K/N\).

## 10. Stopping rule for the next search

The next search remains bounded.

A candidate advances only if it plausibly contains all three structural layers:

### BEFORE
A defensible prior-performance measure observed before the peer environment.

### DURING
An identifiable environment with measurable peers and meaningful exposure.

### AFTER
A real, scarce downstream opportunity with observed selection.

If no public candidate meets those conditions after a bounded search, report that result explicitly. At that point, restricted-use acquisition can be reconsidered with a much clearer justification.

## 11. Standing Romania interpretation for future writing

A concise description to preserve:

> Romania shows that access to a stronger educational environment can improve later academic performance while simultaneously worsening students' relative standing and producing measurable adverse behavioral responses. This supports treating environmental benefits and drawbacks as coexisting mechanisms. It does not, however, provide an observed scarce downstream selection event or separately identify the performance penalty attributable to the adverse channel. Romania is therefore retained as supporting mechanism evidence rather than a full congestion-and-selection replication.
