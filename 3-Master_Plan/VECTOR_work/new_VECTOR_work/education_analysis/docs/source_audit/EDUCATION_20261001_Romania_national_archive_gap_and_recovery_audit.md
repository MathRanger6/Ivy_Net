## What we learned

1. **The interrupted national run is incomplete, but its saved work is intact.** The official directory lists 41 county units. At the stopped checkpoint, 30 had county manifests, 10 of those were complete, and 11 had no manifest yet. The 30 manifests recorded 20 page addresses returning HTTP 404 and 10 returning HTTP 429. These numbers describe archive retrieval, not a research sample.
2. **A 404 is not simply evidence that we requested an invented page.** All 20 original 404 addresses were linked from saved, verified report pages. A paced lookup through the Internet Archive's Availability API reported no alternative capture for those exact addresses. This is evidence of a source-archive gap, though an empty API answer does not prove that every possible recovery route is exhausted.
3. **A 429 is a different problem.** It means the request was throttled. One targeted retry of a previously throttled Bistrița-Năsăud admitted-placement page returned 404 instead; its manifest now records that fixed gap. At that point, the manifests contained 21 HTTP 404 and nine HTTP 429 entries.
4. **Five of those nine throttled addresses had reported captures.** We retried only those five, using the existing archive client with 60-second request spacing. All five returned source pages, passed source-path, table-column, and checkpoint-integrity checks, and were saved in the private cache. Their report-view row counts were 500 (Gorj candidate roster), 194 (Galați unassigned applicants), 500 (Iași admitted placements), 500 (Maramureș admitted placements), and 500 (Neamț admitted placements): **2,194 rows across report views**, not 2,194 newly identified students. The remaining four 429 addresses had no capture reported by the Availability API; they have not been declared irrecoverable.
5. **Recovered pages do not make their counties complete.** Four of the five recovered pages link onward to another page not yet saved: Gorj candidate page index 3001, Iași and Maramureș admitted page index 1500, and Neamț admitted page index 2500. Galați's recovered unassigned report has no further page in that family, but its admitted-placement family still has a 404 gap. The saved county manifests are not yet refreshed to incorporate these five new page checkpoints; the normal acquisition notebook will rebuild them on resume.
6. **Redundant school-specific reports look promising as a bounded repair route.** The official origin-school directories list 248 schools in Bihor and 119 in Brăila. A two-page source pilot recovered one Bihor school candidate report with 47 rows, all 47 matching records already present in the saved Bihor county candidate report on name and admission average. A Brăila school placement report had 74 rows, 42 matching its saved county placement report; the other 32 are *unpaired with the currently saved county report*, not yet proven missing from the source universe. This establishes an overlapping report view, not completeness or a validated national reconstruction. The 17 counties with original 404 gaps list 3,249 origin-school codes in total, so a blanket school-report crawl would be a substantial new acquisition, not a small retry.

## Why this matters for the scientific question

An apparent change in placement rates could be caused by missing county pages or an incomplete originating-gymnasium applicant pool. We therefore cannot treat the presently saved national files as a national research cohort. The previously completed **Alba 2001** analyses have their own documented aperture and are not invalidated by this national acquisition gap. No new national curve, sorting estimate, or selection comparison follows from this audit.

We should also avoid assuming that a county candidate count must equal that county's admitted-plus-unassigned count: students can cross county boundaries. The identity-level reconciliation must use the saved source records, rather than count equality alone, and must remain within the private cache.

An independent occupancy-total check below shows that the 20 placement gaps are **genuinely missing admission rows**; whether and how many rows are missing at the candidate-roster and unassigned-applicant gaps remains unresolved. The name-free address inventory, including the referring page's actual Wayback capture date, is `EDUCATION_20261001_Romania_unresolved_page_inventory.csv` in this directory.

**Independent placement completeness check.** Each county's saved program-occupancy report has a `Candidaţi admişi` count for each program. Summing those counts gives 176,159 admitted students across the 41 county units. The saved county placement reports contain 132,463 rows, a **43,696-row shortfall**. Crucially, the occupancy total exactly equals the placement-row count in **all 21 counties without a placement-page 404**, while **all 20 counties with such a 404** have a positive shortfall (67 to 18,969 rows). This pattern is strong direct evidence that the missing placement pages are not merely empty terminal links. The count comparison is within destination-county reports, so cross-county applicants do not explain these placement shortfalls. This check says nothing by itself about candidate-roster completeness or the final national research population.

The name-free county-by-county audit is `EDUCATION_20261001_Romania_placement_occupancy_reconciliation.csv` in this directory.

The earlier Availability API audit reported no alternative capture for the first 20 missing addresses. The subsequent paced **Wayback capture-index audit checked all 40 exact addresses for successful (`HTTP 200`) captures across dates and found none indexed**. Three transient lookup errors were retried and resolved; the result file records all attempts and final outcomes. A positive-control query using a known saved Teleorman candidate page at index 2501 returned its 2003 and 2004 successful captures, confirming that this exact-URL query method can find archived pages with the same filename structure. A direct request for Teleorman's missing candidate page at the capture date of its neighboring saved page returned 404. This is strong evidence against recovering the *exact requested URLs* from another dated Wayback save, but it does not rule out URL variants, omitted captures, or redundant school-specific reports. Nearby *saved* pages have captures spanning 2002–2005; those dates alone do not establish a capture of a missing address. The index audit queried metadata only and did not download student tables.

An offline, aggregate-only linkage check found that the printed `CNP` field has the same 13-character placeholder on every saved row; it is **not a usable person identifier**. Using exact printed name plus numerically normalized admission average as a provisional signature, 112,787 candidate signatures uniquely matched one placement signature and 3,151 uniquely matched one unassigned signature. There were 14,554 unique candidate signatures with neither saved outcome, 19,670 unique placement signatures without a saved candidate, and 4,075 unique unassigned signatures without a saved candidate. Two candidate signatures and three placement signatures were internally duplicated; these were not forced into a match. These are **coverage diagnostics, not final person-level reconciliation**. Different county report universes, archive gaps, and score/name transcription differences may contribute. No names or identifiers were exported to this document.

## Earlier October 1 operational pacing trial — historical record

Charles observed that roughly one-minute waits before both the initial Wayback request and its redirect made a successfully saved page take about two minutes. A local review of the last 100 logged HTTP attempts found 45 successful pages, 47 redirects, four missing captures, four network errors, and no HTTP 429 responses. This supports testing a faster pace, but does not establish a safe universal rate for the Archive.

The acquisition companion then specified a **bounded trial**: cap the saved starting target interval at **30 seconds per HTTP request** and stop after **50 HTTP attempts**, counting redirects. The ordinary 60-second adaptive maximum, the Archive's `Retry-After` instruction, and the minimum five-minute backoff after a 429 remained active. Previously saved checkpoints and any future server-requested pause remained intact. The new `NEW SOURCE SAVED` output distinguished actual new downloads from previously saved pages. The notebook itself was not edited. Offline checks passed for the restart cap, 50-attempt stop behavior, and 429 backoff; **no live archive request or substantive analysis was made while implementing this trial**.

That bounded trial was subsequently run by Charles. The 50-attempt cap was then removed at his request. Current acquisition code uses a 10-second minimum target interval, a random directional change of up to 10% after completed pages or negative feedback, and explicit five-minute minimum backoff after an HTTP 429. The preceding paragraph records the earlier code state; it is not a current instruction to stop at 50 attempts.
