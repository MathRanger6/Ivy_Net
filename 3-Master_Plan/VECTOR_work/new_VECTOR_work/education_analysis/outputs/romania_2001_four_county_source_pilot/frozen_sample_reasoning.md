# Romania 2001 admissions records: why we checked individual gymnasiums

## 1. What these records describe

In 2001, students leaving Romanian lower-secondary schools (called *gymnasiums* here) participated in a computerized process that assigned applicants to upper-secondary schools and programs. A *program* here means a course of study at a particular upper-secondary school, such as a mathematics or language track. The Romanian Ministry of Education published webpages showing the applicants and placements on its admissions website, `www.edu.ro/adm2001`. We downloaded historical copies of those Ministry webpages from the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine. **Every report discussed below is an archived copy of a Ministry webpage. None came from a separate county or gymnasium website.**

The **three Ministry views used in our applicant comparison** are named below. These names are our labels for the archived webpages, not the Ministry's official report titles:

1. **County Applicant View:** A Ministry webpage whose table lists applicants for the county selected in that webpage's URL. Each table row shows one applicant's printed name, originating gymnasium name, national examination average, average of grades earned in school years 5–8, and composite admission average. The composite combines the examination and school-grade averages; the inspected 2001 Alba records follow a 75% examination / 25% school-grades calculation. For example, the **URL address** of an Alba County Applicant View contains `raport_candidati_total.asp-cj=AB&nj=ALBA`: `AB` is the county code and `ALBA` is the county name. This is an excerpt from a webpage URL address, not a student-data field. Another part of the URL identifies which page of the county's multi-page view is displayed.
2. **Gymnasium Directory View:** A Ministry webpage listing originating gymnasium names, the school codes assigned by the Ministry, and links to Ministry webpages showing applicants associated with each coded gymnasium. We used those links and codes; we did not invent them.
3. **Gymnasium Applicant View:** A Ministry webpage filtered to applicants associated with one originating gymnasium. For example, the **URL address** of the Gymnasium Applicant View for Galați gymnasium code 154 contains `raport_candidati_per_scoala.asp-cj=GL&nj=GALATI&cs=154`. This is also an excerpt from a webpage URL address: `GL` and `GALATI` identify the county, while `cs=154` identifies the gymnasium. “Gymnasium Applicant View” describes the filter used by the Ministry webpage, **not a webpage published by the school**.

The Ministry site also published **other webpages**, used here to check what happened to applicants rather than to choose the 36 gymnasiums. We call a page listing students assigned to an upper-secondary school and program a **Placement Report**. We call a page listing applicants who received no place an **Unassigned Applicant Report**. A **Program Occupancy Summary** states how many places were filled in each program. A Placement Report is therefore a **fourth kind of Ministry webpage**, not one of the three applicant-source views above; the Unassigned Applicant Report and Program Occupancy Summary are additional Ministry records. We name them here so none appears later without an introduction.

The County Applicant View and a Gymnasium Applicant View may show the **same applicant**. We must identify that overlap, not add the two row counts together. They may also disagree because the views cover different county-defined sets of applicants, because the same gymnasium name is printed differently, or because some archived webpages are missing. We first checked those possible source problems before using the applicant rows to describe a gymnasium's peer group.

## 2. Why we made this check

Our proposed study would group applicants by the gymnasium they attended, describe how well other applicants from that gymnasium performed, and examine who was placed in a selective upper-secondary program. If the County Applicant View omits applicants—or if our code fails to connect a gymnasium name printed in that view to the correct school code in the Gymnasium Directory View—our reconstructed gymnasium group may be wrong. We could then calculate the wrong peer-performance average or divide successful placements by the wrong number of applicants.

An earlier check retrieved a Gymnasium Applicant View for each of the **168 gymnasium codes in Alba's Gymnasium Directory View**. Those Gymnasium Applicant Views showed **78 student-name-and-admission-score appearances across 44 gymnasiums** that our comparison had not assigned to the same gymnasiums from Alba's County Applicant View. Ministry Placement Reports also showed those 78 appearances, consistent with some students appearing in placement records outside the county-defined applicant view. These are appearances across webpages, not necessarily 78 distinct students. We checked three more counties before deciding whether Alba's pattern was unusual.

## 3. Which additional schools we checked, and why

We selected **Caraș-Severin (CS), Galați (GL), and Tulcea (TL)** for the bounded check; Alba was already audited. In each of the three counties we chose **12 originating gymnasiums** and downloaded their 36 Gymnasium Applicant Views. Twelve was a practical spot-check size, **not** the result of a statistical sample-size calculation and **not** intended to estimate a county-wide missing-record percentage.

We chose the gymnasiums *before* downloading their Gymnasium Applicant Views and saved the chosen school codes in `frozen_school_report_sample.csv` (a comma-separated-values file that a spreadsheet program can open). This is what “frozen” means here: we did not switch schools after viewing the comparison results. For each county, the selection included:

- **2 ordinary gymnasiums** to which our strict matching of County Applicant View names against the Gymnasium Directory View assigned no applicants;
- **3 schools** from the lower third of positive matched-applicant counts;
- **4 schools** from the middle third; and
- **3 schools** from the upper third.

To make the three size groups, we sorted gymnasiums by how many County Applicant View rows our school-name matching method attributed to each, then divided the positive-count gymnasiums into three groups with roughly equal numbers of gymnasiums. These groups are based on **applicant counts**, not examination scores. We excluded clubs and special schools from the zero-count group. Within each size group, a computer selected gymnasiums randomly. The *random seed*—the number supplied to the computer's random-choice routine—was `20261001` plus a fixed value derived from the county code, so rerunning the code makes the same choices. Exam scores and placements played no part in choosing gymnasiums. A zero *matched* count means only that our method attributed no County Applicant View rows to that gymnasium code. It does **not** prove that no student from that gymnasium appears anywhere in the County Applicant View.

## 4. What the comparison actually did

First, the code read previously saved County Applicant Views and Gymnasium Directory Views. It compared the gymnasium name printed beside each applicant in a County Applicant View with gymnasium names in that county's Gymnasium Directory View. It ignored only differences in capital letters, accents, punctuation, and spaces. It attached a Ministry school code to a County Applicant View row **only when the complete adjusted name identified exactly one gymnasium in the Gymnasium Directory View**. If a name was shortened, could describe multiple gymnasiums, or named a gymnasium in another county, the code left that row for review instead of guessing.

Next, for each of the 36 selected gymnasium codes, we downloaded its Gymnasium Applicant View. The code compared each row in that Gymnasium Applicant View with County Applicant View rows already attributed to the same gymnasium code. It used the **printed student name and composite admission average together** to identify a possible match. It counted rows found in both views, rows found only in the Gymnasium Applicant View, and rows attributed to that gymnasium only from the County Applicant View. This pairing is provisional: names plus scores are not unique national student IDs, and duplicate combinations would require further review.

Downloaded copies of the archived Ministry webpages, including student names and scores, stay in `~/Desktop/VECTOR_temp/romania_2001_four_county_school_source_pilot/`, outside the synced research repository. The repository contains the predetermined school-code list and **counts without student names or scores** in `sample_source_comparison_counts.csv`.

## 5. What the completed check found

The notebook downloaded **all 36 selected Gymnasium Applicant Views**: 12 for originating gymnasiums in each of Caraș-Severin, Galați, and Tulcea. Before using a saved webpage, the code checked its source webpage URL address, number of table rows, and file checksum (a digital fingerprint used to detect an altered saved file). “Verified” here refers to those file checks, **not** to independent proof that the Ministry's view included every eligible student. Every County Applicant View row that our strict school-name method attributed to one of the 36 gymnasiums appeared in the corresponding Gymnasium Applicant View. The Gymnasium Applicant Views also showed **37 student-name-and-admission-score appearances that our method had not assigned to those gymnasiums from the County Applicant Views**: **24 in Caraș-Severin, 7 in Galați, and 6 in Tulcea**. Those 37 appearances are not a proven count of distinct omitted students or an estimate for whole counties.

One Galați example makes the distinction concrete. The Galați Gymnasium Directory View identifies gymnasium code 154 as `SCOALA BALASESTI`. Our exact school-name matching procedure attributed **zero rows from the saved Galați County Applicant View** to that code. The **Gymnasium Applicant View for code 154** displayed **one applicant row**. We have not yet established whether that applicant is absent from the Galați County Applicant View or appears there under a school name that our strict rule failed to connect to code 154. The observation is **zero attributed by our matching procedure versus one in the Gymnasium Applicant View**. It is not a disagreement between a county website and a school website.

## 6. What this establishes—and what the offline follow-up found

The check establishes that **our present method—taking County Applicant View rows and attaching gymnasium codes only through exact matches to the Gymnasium Directory View—fails to reproduce every applicant shown in the 36 sampled Gymnasium Applicant Views**. We must investigate both which applicants each County Applicant View covers and which gymnasium names our code cannot link before calling a reconstructed gymnasium group complete. The check does **not** establish that all 37 appearances are absent from the County Applicant Views. It also does not tell us how many eighth graders attended any gymnasium or whether peer strength affected anyone's upper-secondary placement.

On October 2, we ran a **read-only check of the already downloaded webpages**. The code searched for each of the 37 printed-name-and-admission-average pairs **anywhere** in the corresponding county's complete County Applicant View, without requiring a gymnasium-name match. It found **none of the 37**. Thus a simple difference in how a gymnasium name was printed does not explain these 37 pairs under this exact person-matching rule.

The code then searched County Applicant Views already saved for the **other counties**. It found **24 of the 37 pairs** in another county's County Applicant View: 14 from Caraș-Severin gymnasiums, 7 from Galați gymnasiums, and 3 from Tulcea gymnasiums. The remaining **13 pairs** did not appear in any saved County Applicant View. Searching the other counties' saved Placement Reports and Unassigned Applicant Reports found **all 13**: 10 associated with Caraș-Severin gymnasiums and 3 with Tulcea gymnasiums. Of these 13 pairs, 10 appeared in Placement Reports and 3 appeared in Unassigned Applicant Reports. Two of the 10 placed pairs appeared in both a county-level Placement Report and a program-level Placement Report; these are overlapping records, not two people. None of the 13 appeared in both placed and unassigned reports.

These results give an **existing-record lead for all 37 pairs**, with no new web requests. A pair consists of a printed name and an admission average; an exact match is strong evidence of correspondence but is **not a unique national student identifier**. The check establishes where matching text occurs. It does not yet certify 37 distinct identities or a fully reconstructed gymnasium cohort. The pattern is consistent with county boundaries affecting which applicants appear in each County Applicant View, but we must verify the meaning of each report's county filter before labeling every case a cross-county move or placement. Even a complete group of applicants in this admission process would not necessarily include every student who attended or graduated from a gymnasium.

A separate computer check inspected saved webpages for **41 county-coded jurisdictions in this archive collection**. In each of the 41, the number of students shown in recovered Placement Reports agrees with the filled-place total in the Program Occupancy Summary. All expected webpages making up the County Applicant Views are saved in 22 of the 41. All expected Unassigned Applicant Report webpages are saved in 40. These counts tell us which **webpages** we recovered and cross-checked. They do not prove that we have every gymnasium's full student body or even every participant in every Ministry view. We did not use these checks to calculate a national placement curve or estimate an effect of congestion.

While downloading the 36 Gymnasium Applicant Views, the Wayback server returned **HTTP 429**, the server code for “Too Many Requests,” after the notebook had saved 26 pages. The 26 saved pages remained on disk, and the notebook later completed the remaining downloads. The notebook normally spaces Gymnasium Applicant View web requests by 22–50 seconds. If the server returns 429, the notebook prints the **exact requested URL address** and the full response headers and body, waits at least 600 seconds, and retries that page. A second consecutive 429 triggers a 1,500-second wait and one more retry; a third stops the download pass. The notebook saves the time when it may request again, so restarting it does not erase the wait. If the server explicitly asks for a longer wait in its `Retry-After` response header, the longer wait applies. These settings belong to the 36-page Gymnasium Applicant View check, not the separate national download program.
