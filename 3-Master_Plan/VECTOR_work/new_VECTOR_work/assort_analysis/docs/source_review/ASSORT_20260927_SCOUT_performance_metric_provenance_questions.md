**Prepared:** September 27, 2026

**Purpose:** Decide whether existing basketball measures are credible proxies for an individual's selection-relevant quality, while keeping team context and eventual selection outcomes conceptually separate.

## Why this question matters

Our current points-per-minute measure may blend player quality with offensive role and opportunity. Charles's scientific aim is eventually to distinguish what a player brings from what a team environment does to that player. For the immediate dissertation work, we need a defensible measure of a quality that could matter to selection; we do **not** need to solve latent talent, optimize a draft curve, or launch a broad search for new measures.

The bounded comparison is **season points per minute (PPM), college Box Plus/Minus (BPM), and Player Efficiency Rating (PER)** on the same accepted 2015 player population. These are candidates to understand, not candidates to rank by whichever produces the strongest sorting index or most appealing outcome curve. In particular, a high team-sorting value for BPM could partly follow from BPM's own team-performance adjustment rather than revealing independent evidence of talent sorting.

The accepted PPM values were built from season totals of game-box points and minutes after source recovery and eligibility decisions. The source and decisions are in `3-Master_Plan/VECTOR_work/new_VECTOR_work/assort_analysis/docs/decisions/ASSORT_20260927_rotation_audit_decisions.md` and the earlier construction specification in that `docs/decisions/` folder.

VECTOR made a **read-only, provisional** join of those 4,267 accepted player records to `datasets/mbb/DO_NOT_ERASE/bpm_player_season_matched.csv` using `(athlete_id, season, team_id)`. It found 4,176 key matches, 4,174 finite BPM values, 4,163 finite PER values, and 4,161 players with both finite values. Thus BPM has approximately **97.8%** coverage and PER approximately **97.6%** coverage on this population. The 2015 matched-file subset had 4,667 rows and no duplicate three-field keys. The 93 players without finite BPM had a median accepted season playing time of 602 minutes, versus 535.5 among those with finite BPM. **That observation does not explain why the matches are missing**; it warns us against assuming they are just brief participants. These counts establish availability only. They do not validate identities, measure meanings, or sample comparability.

Relevant implementation: `sports/sports_pipeline/perf_metric.py` maps metric names to columns; `sports/sports_pipeline/panel_build.py` copies a selected measure to `perf`; `sports/sports_pipeline/panel_rebuild.py` left-joins matched Sports-Reference columns by the three-field key; and `sports/sports_pipeline/bpm_merge.py` constructs the matched file from normalized player name, school slug, and season, with a rule for duplicate source records. The saved matched file may reflect an earlier panel or matching run. Please verify its lineage rather than assuming it belongs to the newly rebuilt 2015 population.

The older `3-Master_Plan/re_entry/HEROs_and_PASSes/sports_sandbox/_DISPOSABLE_perf_metric_rho_eda/H_SORT_LADDER_REPORT.md` reports pooled sorting-index values of about 0.064 for PPM, 0.337 for BPM, and 0.111 for PER over a broader period. Its metric-specific sample sizes differ. Those values must **not** be read as a controlled comparison on the accepted 2015 population. The related `loo_shape/LOO_SHAPE_REPORT.md` asks a different question about draft-curve shape. Neither the sorting ladder nor the curve-shape gate, on its own, establishes that a metric represents portable player quality.

Sports-Reference's [college BPM 2.0 announcement](https://www.sports-reference.com/blog/2020/05/bpm-2-0-on-college-basketball-reference/) and [BPM 2.0 methodology](https://www.basketball-reference.com/about/bpm2.html) explain that BPM uses player box statistics **and team overall performance**. Its team adjustment aligns players' minutes-weighted values with team efficiency. The [PER methodology](https://www.basketball-reference.com/about/per.html) describes PER as a per-minute box-production rating adjusted for pace and normalized to a league average. These descriptions motivate the questions below; please check which versions and columns our saved college file actually contains.
