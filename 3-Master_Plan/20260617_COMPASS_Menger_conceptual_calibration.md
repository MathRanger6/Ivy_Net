## One paragraph for manuscript §2 (COMPASS draft — VECTOR may edit)

> Following Menger (2024), we treat talent not as a fixed individual endowment but as an outcome of **selective advancement systems** — tournaments, rankings, and **assortative matching** — that concentrate high performers into local peer environments. Our contribution is not a general theory of inequality amplification, but an empirical and mechanistic account of how **leave-one-out peer-pool quality** relates non-monotonically to advancement across military, athletic, and academic settings, and how **congestion within** those pools may constrain borderline performers even when average peer quality is high. In this sense, the same talent concentrations that organizations construct for development may also intensify competitive constraint.
