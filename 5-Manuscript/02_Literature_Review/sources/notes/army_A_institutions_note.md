# Army A — officer personnel institutions (evaluation, promotion, assignment, DOPMA, internal labor markets): source note

**Purpose:** verbatim, page-cited extracts documenting how U.S. Army officer personnel institutions work, for the dissertation introduction. Not dissertation prose.

**Conventions**

- "p. N" = the page number **printed** on the page (not the PDF index). For regulations, the paragraph number is also given (e.g., "para 3–9a(3)(c)1b)" = paragraph 3–9, subparagraph a, (3), (c), item 1, sub-item b)).
- Quotes are copied from `pdftotext -layout` extractions. Every page number was checked by extracting that single PDF page (`pdftotext -f N -l N`) and reading its printed footer or header. Words broken by end-of-line hyphens are rejoined. In the Army regulations, the extractor puts spaces inside form numbers ("DA Form 67 – 10 – 1"); those spaces are removed here ("DA Form 67–10–1"). Omissions are marked [...]. Bracketed insertions are [editorial].
- Channel tags (assistant judgment, not the source's claim): **[development]** what a person becomes; **[evaluation]** how a given performance is rated or scored; **[selection]** who gets a scarce slot; **[local]** vs **[wide field]** describes the comparison pool.
- "NOT FOUND" = searched for and absent from this source file (search terms listed).

**Contents:** §1 AR 623–3 (2019); §2 AR 600–8–29 (2020); §3 DA PAM 600–3 (2019); §4 Rostker et al. (1993); §5 Doeringer & Piore (1970 ERIC report / 1971 book); §6 Lazear & Oyer (2004); §7 Hicks (1987).

## §1 Department of the Army. (2019). *Army Regulation 623–3: Evaluation Reporting System*. Headquarters, Department of the Army, 14 June 2019.

File: `sources/FOUNDY_Intro/files/139/` (PDF; printed page = PDF index − 8 in the body).

**A5. Which grades use which forms**

- Officer evaluation reports (OERs) come on four "plates" by grade [evaluation] — "DA Form 67–10–1 (Company Grade Plate (O1 – O3; WO1 – CW2) Officer Evaluation Report), DA Form 67–10–2 (Field Grade Plate (O4 – O5; CW3 – CW5) Officer Evaluation Report), DA Form 67–10–3 (Strategic Grade Plate (O6) Officer Evaluation Report), and DA Form 67–10–4 (Strategic Grade Plate General Officer Evaluation Report)" (p. 1, para 1–1).

**A4. Purpose of the OER for selection and other personnel decisions**

- The main job of the evaluation system is to feed Headquarters, Department of the Army (HQDA) decisions [evaluation → selection] — "The primary function of the ERS is to provide information to HQDA for use in making personnel management decisions." (p. 4, para 1–8c(1)).
- Ratings are an input to centralized selection and to assignment — "Rating chains' views of performance and/or potential for use in centralized selection, assignment, and other personnel management." (p. 4, para 1–8c(1)(d)).
- No single report is meant to decide a career — "An appraisal philosophy that recognizes a single evaluation report will not normally determine a Soldier's Army career ("whole file" concept) [...] will best serve the Army and the rated Soldier." (p. 4, para 1–8c(1)(c)).
- Raters must discriminate among officers so that boards can choose — "rating officials are obligated to the Army to be accurate and discriminating in their evaluations so Army leaders, HQDA selection boards, and career managers can make informed decisions." (p. 34, para 3–2f).
- "Potential" is defined as relative to others of the same grade [evaluation] — "Assessments of potential will be performance-based assessments of rated officers' or NCOs' ability to perform in positions of greater responsibility and/or higher grades compared to others of the same grade." (p. 5, para 1–9c).

**A2. Rater-level constraint ("Excels")**

- The rater compares the officer to the other officers of the same rank whom that rater has rated [evaluation; local] — "This overall performance assessment is evaluated in terms of the rated officer compared against the total population of officers, in the same rank, the rater previously rated or currently rates in their population." (p. 39, para 3–7a(3)(c)).
- The top rater box is meant for roughly the top third — "The intent is for the rater to use this box to identify the upper third of officers for each rank." (p. 39, para 3–7a(3)(c)).
- The cap is under 50% "Excels" per rank, and a report that breaks the cap is relabeled — "In order to maintain a credible profile, the rater must have less than 50 percent of the ratings of each rank in the "Excels" box. OERs with an "Excels" rating that causes a rater's profile to have 50 percent or more "Excels" ratings will be processed with a "Proficient" HQDA electronically generated label (see DA Pam 623–3); however, it will be charged against the rater's profile as an "Excels," and a documented rater profile misfire will occur." (p. 39, para 3–7a(3)(c)1).
- The rater cap applies to company- and field-grade reports for lieutenant colonels (LTCs) and below — "Raters for LTCs and below on company and field grade plate reports are required to maintain a rater's profile percentage of less than 50 percent for rendered OERs, separated by grade in order for the final completed OER to maintain the "Excels" HQDA label." (p. 47, para 3–11a(5)).
- Raters keep no profile for colonels (COLs) — "Raters do not maintain a profile on officers in the ranks of COL and above." (p. 46, para 3–11a).
- New raters get a cushion — "the rater will be given a one-time profile credit of three "Proficient" box checks, for each rated rank, the first time they rate a specific rank." (p. 39, para 3–7a(3)(c)5).

**A1 + A3. Senior rater profile constraint ("Most Qualified") and the comparison pool**

- The senior rater compares the officer's potential to all officers of the same rank in that senior rater's past and current population [evaluation; local] — "the senior rater will assess the rated officer's potential compared to all officers of the same rank. This assessment should be based on officers the senior rater has previously senior rated and those in their current senior rater population." (p. 42, para 3–9a(3)(c)1).
- The top box is meant for the upper third — "The intent is for the senior rater to use this box to identify the upper third of officers for each rank." (p. 42, para 3–9a(3)(c)1a)).
- **The cap is under 50% "Most Qualified" per rank, per senior rater. Above the cap, the report is relabeled "Highly Qualified" but still counts as "Most Qualified" in the profile (a "misfire")** — "In order to maintain a credible profile, the senior rater must have less than 50 percent of the ratings of a rank in the "Most Qualified" top box. Fifty percent or more in the "Most Qualified" ratings will be processed with a "Highly Qualified" HQDA electronically generated label (see DA Pam 623–3); however, it will be charged against the senior rater's profile as a "Most Qualified" OER if it is unresolved, and a documented senior rater profile misfire will occur." (p. 43, para 3–9a(3)(c)1b)).
- One-third is advice, not a rule — "Senior raters may consider limiting the use of the "Most Qualified" rating box check to roughly one-third of all ratings for officers of a given rank, but this is not a requirement." (p. 43, para 3–9a(3)(c)1b)).
- New senior raters: one "Most Qualified" in the first four — "For senior raters first establishing a profile (separate by rank), only one of the first four OERs received for processing at HQDA for any given grade may be rated as "Most Qualified."" (p. 43, para 3–9a(3)(c)1e)).
- The cap is separate for each grade, for second lieutenant (2LT) through LTC — "senior raters of 2LT through LTC officers are required to maintain a profile percentage of less than 50 percent for rendered OERs, separated by grade." (p. 48, para 3–11c(5), Note).
- Colonels (DA Form 67–10–3) have a two-box cap — "senior raters must maintain a cumulative percentage of the upper two boxes combined ("Multi-Star Potential" and "Promote to BG") of less than 50 percent of the ratings for a given rank and/or the "Multi-Star Potential" box having less than 24 percent of the ratings for a given rank." (p. 43, para 3–9a(3)(c)2b)).
- The label depends on the profile **at the moment the report is processed**, so a report's label depends on the other reports already in the same senior rater's pool [evaluation; local] — "All OERs will receive an HQDA electronically generated label that reflects the senior rater's profile at the time the OER processes, based on the date of receipt." (p. 43, para 3–9a(3)(c)3).
- Processing order is arrival order — "Within a senior rater's profile, HQDA will always process OERs and NCOERs daily in the order received, based on the date and time of receipt, regardless of the "Thru" date of the OER or NCOER." (p. 48, para 3–11c(1)(b)).
- A promotable officer ("P") serving in a higher-grade job is pooled with the next higher rank — "On Senior Rater Profile reports, they will be profiled against the next higher rank." (p. 43, para 3–9a(3)(c)1c)).
- The profile follows the senior rater from job to job, so the pool is cumulative and not tied to one unit — "Continue without interruption as the senior rater (in either a military or civilian status) moves from job to job." (p. 48, para 3–11c(1)(d)).
- Boards see the size of the senior rater's pool and can discount small pools [evaluation → selection] — "This information, in conjunction with additional information contained on the HQDA electronically generated label, will help HQDA selection boards identify senior raters with small rating populations and weigh the report accordingly." (p. 43, para 3–9a(3)(c)6).
- The profile history exists to discipline the ratings that boards see — "Provide information to HQDA selection boards and the Army leadership on the senior rater's profile history as a means of disciplining the rating system." (p. 48, para 3–11c(1)(c)).

**Limits/caveats:** This is the 14 June 2019 edition, and the rules above are for the DA Form 67–10 series. Earlier OER forms and later editions may use different box names and caps, so match the edition to the years of any data. The regulation names the rating pool only as each rater's or senior rater's population of the same rank. It does not say how the pool is formed or how large it usually is. "Misfire" is used as above; the regulation says nothing about how often misfires happen.

## §2 Department of the Army. (2020). *Army Regulation 600–8–29: Officer Promotions*. Headquarters, Department of the Army, 9 September 2020.

File: `sources/FOUNDY_Intro/files/710/` (PDF; printed page = PDF index − 6 in the body).

**B1. Selection is centralized (HQDA boards), except for 1LT/CW2**

- Centralized selection for nearly all grades [selection; wide field] — "The Army will provide a centralized selection process for promotion to all grades except GEN, LTG, 1LT, and CW2." (p. 2, para 1–7).
- Only promotion to first lieutenant (1LT) and chief warrant officer two (CW2) is local — "Commanders in the grade of LTC or above, or frocked to the grade of LTC, are authorized to promote officers to the grade of 1LT and CW2." (p. 2, para 1–7).

**B2. Zones of consideration (defined by date of rank, DOR)**

- Eligibility comes from date of rank and time in grade — "For centralized promotions, eligibility is based on an officer's ADOR and time in grade (TIG)." (p. 8, para 2–7b). [ADOR = active date of rank.]
- Promotion (primary) zone — "A promotion eligibility category (defined by an announced range of DOR) consisting of commissioned officers and warrant officers on the ADL of the same rank and competitive category who are eligible for promotion consideration who— a. As LTCs or below, are eligible for promotion consideration for the first time (excluding any BZ consideration)." (p. 56, Glossary, "Promotion zone"). [ADL = active duty list.]
- Above the zone (AZ) — "[...] whose DOR is senior to any officer in the promotion zone." (p. 53, Glossary, "Above the zone").
- Below the zone (BZ) — "[...] whose date of grade is junior to any officer in the promotion zone." (p. 54, Glossary, "Below the zone").
- The full zone — "The zone of consideration consists of the promotion zone, AZ, and BZ." (p. 56, Glossary, "Zone of consideration").
- The term "year group" — **NOT FOUND** in AR 600–8–29 (searched "year group"). Zones are defined by DOR. The year-group language is in DA PAM 600–3 (§3).

**B3. Who sets the number selected; below-the-zone limit**

- The Secretary of the Army (SECARMY) instructs each board through a memorandum of instruction (MOI), and the maximum is fixed once the board meets — "The SECARMY will provide guidance and instructions in a memorandum of instruction (MOI) to the board. [...] once the board has convened, the maximum number of officers to be selected may not be increased without the written permission of the SECDEF." (p. 5, para 2–6a).
- The MOI states the cap for each competitive category — "The maximum number of officers to be selected from each competitive category." (p. 5, para 2–6a(1)(e)).
- **Below-the-zone (BZ) cap: 10%, or up to 15% with SECDEF approval** — "The number of officers recommended for promotion from BZ may not exceed 10 percent of the total number the board is authorized to select in each competitive category; the SECDEF may authorize that percentage to be increased to no more than 15 percent (10 USC 616)." (p. 9, para 2–8a).
- The board need not fill the BZ maximum — "Unless specified, the maximum number need not be selected." (p. 9, para 2–8e).
- The bar for BZ is relative to the in-zone and above-zone officers — "Officers selected from BZ must be truly outstanding and clearly superior to those who would otherwise be selected from in or above the promotion zone." (p. 9, para 2–8c).
- Stated purpose of BZ — "They are intended to provide officers of exceptional ability an opportunity to advance quickly to more responsible positions, help retain high quality officers, and give officers an incentive to perform at their highest potential." (p. 9, para 2–8b).
- Missing BZ selection is not a pass-over — "failure to be selected will neither count as a nonselection for promotion nor reflect unfavorably on an officer." (p. 9, para 2–8d).

**B4. What the board sees; best vs. fully qualified**

- The file given to the board [evaluation → selection] — "The performance folder of the AMHRR, including authorized documents not in the MBF, the DA Form 4037, an official DA photograph, if available, and approved separation documents." (p. 6, para 2–6b(1)(b)). [AMHRR = Army Military Human Resource Record. The 2020 regulation uses AMHRR. "OMPF" (official military personnel file) appears only in naming the "OMPF custodian" (p. 6, para 2–6b(2)(b)) and as a glossary abbreviation.]
- AMHRR defined — "The permanent, historical, and official record of a Soldier's military service, composed of a performance section, service section, and, in some cases, a restricted access section." (p. 54, Glossary).
- OERs go to the board — "Officers in the zone of consideration will have their evaluation reports provided to promotion boards under the following conditions:" (p. 7, para 2–6d).
- Every officer in the zones is considered — "Base their recommendations on impartial consideration of all officers in the zones of consideration as instructed in the MOI." (p. 9, para 2–9a(1)).
- "Fully qualified" applies when there are at least as many slots as candidates — "The "fully qualified" method will be used when the maximum number of officers authorized to be selected is equal to or greater than the number of available officers from above, in, and below the promotion zone." (p. 9, para 2–9a(3)(a)).
- **"Best qualified" applies when slots are scarce (a comparative choice)** [selection] — "The "best qualified" method will be used when the maximum number of officers authorized to be selected is fewer than the total number of available officers from above, in, and below the promotion zone. Officers recommended under this method will be fully qualified as a threshold requirement." (p. 9, para 2–9a(3)(b)).
- Majority vote — "A board president has no authority to constrain the board from recommending for promotion those fully qualified officers whom the majority finds best qualified to meet the needs of the Army." (p. 4, para 2–4a).
- An explicit statement that boards "compare officers against each other" is **NOT FOUND** verbatim in AR 600–8–29. The comparison follows from the "best qualified" rule above. DA PAM 600–3 says it directly (§3, para 5–9).

**B5. Order of merit / promotion sequence numbers**

- Default order is seniority, set by promotion sequence number — "Commissioned officers are promoted from promotion lists in order of seniority (shown by the promotion sequence number) as additional officers are needed within each competitive category" (p. 12, para 3–1a).
- Merit-based sequencing, when used, puts the board's order of merit on top — "Commissioned officers selected for promotion on boards that utilized merit based sequencing, pursuant to 10 USC 616 and 617, will be placed higher on the promotion list, in their order of merit as determined by the board's vote, for each competitive category." (p. 12, para 3–1b).
- Sequence number defined — "A number that shows the rank order of officers on a promotion list. It is established by the following sort: a. Current DOR. b. Previous DOR." (p. 56, Glossary; the list continues with service, appointment, birth date, alphabetical order).
- AZ selectees pin on first, then in-zone (IZ), then BZ — "Officers selected for promotion from BZ will be promoted following all officers on their promotion list who were part of the AZ population and those selected from the IZ, but before all officers on the subsequent promotion list." (p. 12, para 3–1a(2)).
- Sequence numbers are published monthly — "Promotion sequence numbers for officers to be promoted will be announced in monthly MILPER messages." (p. 14, para 3–3a).

**Limits/caveats:** This is the 9 September 2020 edition; earlier editions may differ (for example, the "merit based sequencing" provision in para 3–1b should be checked against the edition in force for any data years). The regulation gives the rules but no promotion rates and no actual zone dates. Those come in each year's MOI and MILPER message.

## §3 Department of the Army. (2019). *Department of the Army Pamphlet 600–3: Officer Professional Development and Career Management*. Headquarters, Department of the Army, 3 April 2019.

File: `sources/FOUNDY_Intro/files/880/` (53-page PDF; printed page = PDF index − 6 in the body). **This PDF is the core pamphlet only.** The branch-specific chapters (each branch's KD jobs, timelines) live in the online "Smartbook DA Pam 600–3" and are not in this file.

**C1. Key developmental (KD) assignments**

- KD defined [development] — "A KD position is one that is deemed fundamental to the development of an officer's capabilities in their core branch or FA competencies or deemed critical by the senior Army leadership to provide experience across the Army's strategic mission." (p. 12, para 3–4e).
- KD ties to advancement — "Positions created to address specific emerging missions or capability are most often considered KD for the purposes of career development and advancement." (p. 14, para 3–6a).
- KD time is limited so that officers stay competitive — "In order to maximize an officer's ability to pursue broadening assignments and be competitive for promotion to senior commissioned ranks, it is imperative for officers and commanders to understand and adhere to existing guidance that limits key developmental (KD) assignments." (p. 6, para 2–3b).
- Assignment history is a board factor [development → selection] — "Factors considered include— [...] (5) Assignment history and professional development." (p. 29, para 5–10c).
- An explicit sentence that promotion boards weigh **KD** jobs specifically is **NOT FOUND** in this core PDF (searched "KD", "key developmental", "promotion board"). The closest are para 5–10c(5) above and para 5–10e: "promotion potential will be determined, for the most part, based on an officer's record of performance in their designated branch or FA and the officer's overall performance." (p. 30, para 5–10e). The KD-to-board link is probably in the branch Smartbook chapters (not checked).

**C2. How officers are assigned**

- Assignment branches at HRC assign officers (Officer Personnel Management Directorate) — "After officers graduate from BOLC B, branch assignment officers in Officer Personnel Management Directorate will assign the majority of officers to a branch duty position." (p. 13, para 3–5a(2)).
- Army requirements come first — "Army requirements prioritize the selection of an officer for assignment." (p. 20, para 3–14a).
- Branches decide who fits a position — "the requisition cycles span 6 months, and the assignment branches determine which officers meet the position requirements and are available for the assignment." (p. 20, para 3–14a).
- Officer preferences are an input, not a guarantee — "Officers should frequently update their preference statement for location, type of assignments, personal data, professional development goals, and education and training needs. Career managers may not be able to satisfy all preferences because of dynamic requirements, but they do attempt to satisfy as many as possible." (p. 20, para 3–14d(1)).
- The system balances needs — "The OPMS responds to the mission and requirements of the Army and attempts to balance force structure requirements, officer professional development, and individual needs and preferences of the officer." (p. 9, para 3–2f).
- "Marketplace", "Assignment Interactive Module" (AIM), "AIM2", "ATAP" — **NOT FOUND** (searched all four). The pamphlet mentions "The Army's Talent Management Strategy" (p. 3, para 1–8b) but does not describe a market-style assignment system.

**C3. Time-based career model (year groups, uniform timing)**

- Officers move through selection as date-of-rank cohorts ("year groups") [selection; wide field] — "Officers generally flow through the centralized selection subsystem by groupings based on date of rank. Company and field grade officer groupings are termed cohort year groups." (p. 10, para 3–3a(5)).
- Centralized selection is the hub — "The hub around which all the subsystems revolve is centralized selection." (p. 10, para 3–3a(5)).
- Advancement is relative; the OER is the measure [evaluation → selection] — "The Army officer structure is pyramidal. [...] Advancement to increasingly responsible positions is based on relative measures of performance and potential. The mechanism to judge the value of an individual's performance and potential is the OER" (p. 10, para 3–3a(4)).
- Officers compete within their competitive category — "Officers in the same competitive category will compete among themselves for promotion." (p. 29, para 5–9).
- Centralized boards and best-qualified rules — "Officers promoted from captain through colonel and CW3 to CW5 are selected by HQDA centralized boards. [...] When the number of officers being considered exceeds the maximum number to promote, the boards operate under best qualified criteria." (p. 29, para 5–10c).
- Timing to lieutenant colonel — "Cohort year group officers are generally considered for promotion to lieutenant colonel in their 16th year of service as they enter the primary zone of consideration." (p. 14, para 3–6b).
- Timing to colonel — "Cohort year group officers are normally considered for promotion to colonel in the primary zone in their 21st year of service." (p. 16, para 3–7b).
- BZ is one year early — "Below-the-zone selection is possible, and normally officers will be considered 1 year prior to their primary zone consideration." (p. 14, para 3–6b; same wording p. 16, para 3–7b).
- The primary zone is set from year groups and shaped to keep opportunity steady — "The in-the-zone population, or primary zone, is usually established by the dates the first and last due course officer was promoted from a specific year group." (p. 29, para 5–10a). "This primary zone is accessed into the Army, and at times shaped to achieve a promotion opportunity that is relatively similar over a period of the next 5 years." (p. 29, para 5–10a).
- DOPMA is the legal basis for year-group management — "DOPMA [...] is still the basis for year group management of the company and field grade officer corps." (p. 27, para 5–3b).
- Career span — "The life cycle of a cohort year group spans 30 years of service." (p. 20, para 3–14).
- Promotion timing to **major**: **NOT FOUND** in this core PDF (no "considered for promotion to major in their Nth year" sentence; searched "promotion to major").

**Limits/caveats:** This is the 3 April 2019 edition, core chapters only (53 pp.). Branch KD lists and branch timelines are in the online Smartbook, which is not in this file. The timing statements are "generally"/"normally" norms, not rules.

## §4 Rostker, B., Thie, H., Lacy, J., Kawata, J., & Purnell, S. (1993). *The Defense Officer Personnel Management Act of 1980: A Retrospective Assessment* (R-4246-FMP). RAND.

File: `sources/FOUNDY_Intro/files/867/` (PDF; printed page numbers are in the page headers. Front matter is roman, body is arabic).

**D1. Up-or-out**

- Up-or-out is central — "At the heart of DOPMA is the up-or-out promotion system, with common promotion, separation, and retirement rules [...]" (p. 12).
- "Up" = cohort competition at set years of service [selection; wide field] — "officers move through the system in "cohorts" originally determined by the year of commissioning, and compete for promotion to the next higher grade against other members of the group at set years-of-service (YOS) points." (p. 12).
- "Out" = separation after two pass-overs — "The "out" portion of the "up-or-out" system provides that "officers twice passed over for promotion, after a certain number of years, depending upon their particular grade, are to be separated from active service, and if eligible retired."" (p. 12).
- Three-zone structure [selection] — "In effect, each cohort is looked at for promotion at least three times: a small number are selected early, the majority are selected "on-time" (in the zone or in due course), and a small number are selected late." (p. 13).
- BZ non-selection is not a pass-over — "An officer who is not selected "below the zone" is not considered to have been "passed over" (a promotion nonselect)." (p. 13, n. 15).

**D2. Promotion opportunity and timing goals by grade**

- Table 2.2, "DOPMA Up-or-Out Promotion System for "Due-Course" Officer" (p. 14). Columns are "Promotion Opportunity (percentage promoted from surviving cohort)" and "Promotion Timing (primary zone years of service)". Rows as printed:
  - O-4: "80%" / "10±1"
  - O-5: "70%" / "16±1"
  - O-6: "50%" / "22±1"
  - (O-3: "95%" / "3.5/4"; O-2: "100% if fully qualified" / "2.0")
- What "promotion opportunity" means — "Table 2.2 shows the DOPMA promotion opportunity—the cumulative opportunity for advancement for those who compete for promotion to the next higher grade—for each grade and the promotion window that the authors of DOPMA believed would attract and retain the required number of officers." (p. 13).
- **These were goals, not law** — "DOPMA recommended, but did not make statutory, minimum promotion opportunities for each of the officer field grades and offered guidelines for when an officer should be able to pin on the next higher grade" (p. 13, n. 14).
- Same three numbers in prose — "normal promotion opportunities to O-4, O-5, and O-6 of 80 percent, 70 percent, and 50 percent, respectively, cannot all be sustained." (p. 40; from the authors' Section 4 projections for the Army).
- Actual rates drift from the norms — "to meet congressionally mandated grade tables, actual promotion opportunities and timing will often differ from the DOPMA norm." (p. 17).

**D3. Uniform career management across services**

- Stated aim — "In November 1980, Congress amended Title 10, United States Code, to "make uniform the provisions of law relating to appointment, promotion, separation and retirement of regular commissioned officers of the Army, Navy, Air Force and Marine Corps."" (p. 1).
- Purpose quoted from the House report — "[...] provide reasonably consistent career opportunity among the services." (p. 1).
- One uniform career model — "DOPMA established a common officer management system built around a uniform notion of how military officers should be trained, appointed, promoted, separated, and retired." (p. 7).
- The authors' summary of the paradigm — "it was basically an evolutionary document, extending the existing paradigm (grade controls, promotion opportunity and timing objectives, up-or-out, and consistency across the services) that was established after World War II." (p. v, Summary).

**D4. Grade tables / limits set by law**

- A ceiling by grade — "The law established a ceiling on the number of officers in each grade above O-3" (p. 1).
- The grade table scales with end-strength — "Congress specifies in DOPMA the number of officers it would allow in each field grade above O-3. Known as the officer grade distribution and published in the DOPMA grade table, the distribution varies as a function of total officer end-strength rather than as a fixed percentage of total military end-strength." (pp. 7–8).
- Legal targets, not requirements — "The table is not linked to the manpower requirements and personnel authorization process; it represents legal goals to be met rather than needs to be accommodated." (p. 8).

**Limits/caveats:** Published 1993 and describes DOPMA as of about 1980–1992. The 80/70/50 opportunities and 10/16/22-year timings were planning norms, not statutory rules (n. 14, p. 13). Table 2.3 (p. 15) gives Army FY 1990 counts by zone and is the only data. Later amendments to DOPMA are not covered. The author list is from the RAND catalog record because the title page has no extractable text; the Library of Congress data on the verso reads "Bernard Rostker . . . [et al.]".

## §5 Doeringer, P. B., & Piore, M. J. (1971). *Internal Labor Markets and Manpower Analysis*. Lexington, MA: D.C. Heath. — **File is the pre-publication ERIC report:** ED 048 457, dated "May 1970", 344 pp., prepared for the Office of Manpower Research, U.S. Department of Labor.

File: `sources/FOUNDY_Intro/files/899/` (scanned typescript with OCR text layer; 335 PDF pages).

- **Internal labor market defined** — "The central concept around which the manuscript is organized is that of the internal labor market[,] an administrative unit, such as a manufacturing plant, within which the pricing and allocation of labor is governed by a set of administrative rules and procedures." (ERIC typescript pp. I.1a–I.2; the comma is missing in the OCR).
- Contrasted with the external market — "The internal market, governed by administrative rules, is to be distinguished from the external labor market of conventional economic theory where pricing, allocating, and training decisions are governed directly by economic variables." (ERIC p. I.2).
- **Ports of entry defined** — "These two markets are interconnected, however, and movement between them occurs at certain jobs classifications which constitute ports of entry and exit to and from [an] internal labor market. The remainder of the jobs within the internal market are filled by the promotion or transfer of workers who have already gained entry to it." (ERIC p. ## §6 Lazear, E. P., & Oyer, P. (2004). Internal and external labor markets: A personnel economics approach. *Labour Economics*, 11(4), 527–554. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.labeco.2004.04.001

File: `sources/FOUNDY_Intro/files/694/` (PDF; printed journal pages 527–554).

- Definition — "Internal labor markets are those where workers are hired into entry level jobs and higher levels are filled from within. Wages are determined internally and may be quite free of market pressure." (p. 527, Abstract).
- What an internal market requires — "If an internal labor market exists, then there must be some jobs, presumably at high levels, that are filled almost exclusively through internal promotion and there must be other ports-of-entry jobs, presumably at low levels, that are filled through external hiring." (pp. 529–530).
- **Why promote from within: insider-only tournaments give stronger incentives** [selection; pool size] — "Internal promotion is preferred to external hiring because it provides better incentives for workers at lower levels in the firm. If workers know that they must compete with a large pool of outsiders for a particular job, their incentives are diluted because the chances that their increased effort can affect the probability of winning against such a large pool of competitors are slight." (p. 546). "If the pool is narrowed to insiders, incentives are enriched." (pp. 546–547).
- Promotion from within is the norm at higher levels in their data — "Low levels have significant amounts of external hiring and high levels are dominated by promotion from within." (p. 546).
- But pure internal markets are ruled out for Swedish firms — "the evidence on both wages and internal and external hiring patterns rules out pure internal labor markets." (p. 549).
- Military or army — **NOT FOUND** (searched "military", "army"). The paper is about Swedish private-sector white-collar workers.

**Limits/caveats:** The data are Swedish private-sector white-collar employees from the 1970s–1980s. The authors do not discuss the military. Applying the incentive argument to the Army, a near-pure "closed" internal market with essentially no lateral entry at field grade, is an assistant inference, not the authors' claim.
