## X.1 Why this system fits

Army officer promotion connects evaluations produced within local rating relationships to advancement decisions made across a wider field of candidates. Officers serve in units and receive assessments from their rating chains, while centralized promotion boards compare eligible officers within the relevant competitive categories. The officers who shape an individual’s evaluation environment therefore need not constitute the full population competing for promotion. This separation makes the Army a useful setting for examining how an individual’s evaluated record and surrounding peer environment relate to subsequent advancement.

The analysis focuses on the transition from captain to major. Repeated personnel records identify officers’ ranks, assignments, and observation dates, while officer evaluation reports record assessments made during specified periods of service. Linking these sources allows us to follow officers through the promotion window and relate their recorded advancement to evaluations and peer environments observed during their careers. Cohort and observation-window restrictions determine how much opportunity each officer has had to reach major before the records end.

The Army’s performance measure is itself an organizational evaluation. The proportion of an officer’s senior-rater assessments receiving the highest rating summarizes a record of recognized performance and potential, rather than an independently administered measure of achievement. That distinction is central to this setting: the local evaluation process is part of the relationship we seek to describe. The records allow us to examine how promotion varies with an officer’s own evaluation history and the evaluation histories of officers in the surrounding rating environment, while keeping those quantities distinct.

## X.2 How the data were found

The Army analysis draws on administrative records available through the Army Analytics Group’s Person–Event Data Environment (PDE). These records bring together information needed to connect officers’ evaluated performance, organizational surroundings, and career progression. Their value for this study lies in that connection: promotion records identify advancement, while evaluation records provide information about the assessments and rating relationships accompanying an officer’s career.

Two complementary sources form the basis of the research file. Repeated personnel snapshots record officers’ status at successive observation dates, including their ranks and assignments. Officer evaluation reports document assessments over specified periods of service and identify the rating officials associated with those assessments. Together, these sources permit the construction of career histories that retain both the individual officer and the changing evaluation environment.

The records were originally created for personnel administration rather than for this research question. Constructing the analytical data therefore required linking records across sources, reconciling their observation dates, and defining which officers belonged to the peer environment at a given time. These choices determine what the resulting measures describe and are documented separately from the administrative sources themselves.
