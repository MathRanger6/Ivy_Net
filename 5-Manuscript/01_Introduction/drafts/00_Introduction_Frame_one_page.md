## Central question (agreed 10-28)

**Why should an individual's chance of success be studied as a function of the environment in which that individual performs (the peers and the organization), and not of individual performance alone?**

1. Through what channels can peers and organizations alter the prospects of individuals of comparable performance, and what does each discipline establish, dispute, or leave open about those channels?
2. Why would the *shape* of that relationship, and not only its existence, carry information? (Opposing documented forces make non-monotonicity possible, not necessary.)

**Alex's test for the executive summary (PD50):** it ends with a one-sentence answer to *why this curve and this phenomenon*. It is not an abstract.

## What every disciplinary subsection addresses (2–3 pages each)

1. **The discipline's question and unit of analysis.**
2. **Findings sorted by channel and by source:** development, evaluation, selection; peers vs organization.
3. **How it separates sorting from effects.**
4. **What it leaves open for our question** (stated about the literature reviewed, never "the field ignores"). **Built from specific limitations (Alex, PD51):** how each study measured it, and whether its data were small, its measures imperfect, it covered only one system, it had no model of why the effect occurs, or its model was never fit to data. Combined, these show the hole and how we fill it.

**Storyline entry (Alex, PD51):** big fish in a small pond versus the same fish in a big pond, from education, as a soft opening (first section or second/third): education identified the paradigm; we translate it into a systems framework and test whether it generalizes across domains. The education subsection grows accordingly.

## Working frame: three channels × two sources (proposed; not yet formally agreed)

| Channel | Peers | Organization |
|---|---|---|
| **Development** (what a person becomes) | Model excellence; raise the standard of practice | Coaching, facilities, medical care |
| **Evaluation** (how a given performance is measured or perceived) | Set the comparison standard; share minutes, shots, top ratings | Prestige and visibility shape how scouts, boards, committees read the same performance |
| **Selection** (who wins a scarce slot) | Compete for the same slots | Sets slot count and rules: roster size, rater profiles, tenure lines |

Caveat to carry: the peer mean (team or organization average computed without the person's own measurement) cannot by itself separate peer from organizational contributions (Manski 1993).
