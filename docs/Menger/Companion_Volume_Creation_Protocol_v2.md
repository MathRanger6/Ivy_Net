# Companion Volume Creation Protocol v2.0
## Unified LLM Operating Manual for Building Guided Reading Editions of Scholarly Works

### Origin
This protocol is derived from lessons learned during the creation of a full scholarly companion volume for a complex academic manuscript. It incorporates both formatting requirements and intellectual workflow requirements. The objective is reproducibility.

---

# PART I — MISSION

A Companion Volume is NOT:

- A summary
- A literature review
- A study guide
- A paraphrase

A Companion Volume IS:

A guided intellectual edition of a manuscript that allows a reader to think WITH the paper rather than merely understand what it says.

The reader should feel as though an exceptionally attentive scholar is sitting beside them explaining:

- What the author is doing
- Why it matters
- Which passages deserve underlining
- What assumptions are being made
- Which mechanisms generate the phenomena
- How the ideas transfer beyond the paper

---

# PART II — PRIMARY OBJECTIVE

Maximize:

- Comprehension
- Retention
- Internalization
- Conceptual transfer
- Mechanism extraction
- Research insight generation

Do NOT optimize for brevity.

When uncertain:
Expand rather than compress.

---

# PART III — INPUTS

When available:

1. Source manuscript
2. Companion Volume Creation Protocol
3. Example companion volume(s)
4. Reader research context

Treat example companion volumes as style exemplars.

---

# PART IV — ANALYSIS WORKFLOW

Step 1:
Read the manuscript completely.

Step 2:
Identify coherent conceptual units.

Do NOT default to page boundaries.

Do NOT default to paragraph-by-paragraph commentary.

Group paragraphs into idea blocks.

Step 3:
Identify the first paragraph in each block.

The exact opening text becomes the Anchor.

---

# PART V — ANCHOR RULE

Every section begins with:

Anchor (Original Text):

Follow with the EXACT opening text of the first paragraph being discussed.

Requirements:

- Verbatim
- Exact manuscript text
- Never paraphrased
- Never invented

Purpose:

Enable side-by-side reading.

---

# PART VI — QUOTATION EXTRACTION

Every section should identify:

Key Quotations Worth Underlining

Prioritize quotations that:

- Define concepts
- Reveal mechanisms
- Compress complexity
- Clarify causal relationships
- Expose assumptions
- Contain memorable formulations

Formatting:

BLACK
BOLD

Quotations must remain visually distinct from commentary.

---

# PART VII — COMMENTARY ARCHITECTURE

The Companion Volume uses three voices.

BLACK BOLD:
Direct quotations.

BLUE:
Interpretation.

RED:
Project connections and implications.

---

## BLUE TEXT REQUIREMENTS

Blue text answers:

"What is the author really saying?"

Responsibilities:

- Expand compressed arguments
- Clarify concepts
- Explain assumptions
- Explain implications
- Explain why quotations matter
- Connect sections together

Blue text should generally be more verbose than the manuscript.

Goal:
Conceptual digestion.

---

## RED TEXT REQUIREMENTS

Red text answers:

"Why might this matter?"

Possible topics:

- Research implications
- Theory implications
- Methodological implications
- Organizational implications
- Connections to reader projects
- Alternative interpretations

Red text should be interwoven naturally.

Do NOT isolate all red text at the end.

---

# PART VIII — DENSITY STANDARD

Assume the reader will print the document.

Requirements:

- Narrow margins
- Dense prose
- Minimal whitespace
- Compact formatting

Avoid:

- Presentation slides aesthetics
- Excessive spacing
- One-sentence paragraphs unless necessary

Optimize for annotation.

---

# PART IX — MECHANISM EXTRACTION RULE

For every major section identify:

1. Phenomenon
2. Mechanism
3. Amplification process
4. Assumptions
5. Boundary conditions
6. Alternative explanations

Mechanisms matter more than labels.

---

# PART X — THE UNDERLINE TEST

Every section should contain at least one idea answering:

"What would a thoughtful reader underline?"

If no answer exists:

The section is too shallow.

---

# PART XI — THE FIVE-YEAR TEST

Ask:

"What should the reader still remember five years later?"

Prioritize those ideas.

---

# PART XII — CHAPTER TEMPLATE

Chapter Title

Anchor (Original Text)

Key Quotations Worth Underlining

Integrated Commentary
{BLUE and RED (and even BLACK BOLD quotations when appropriate) interwoven naturally}

Margin Notes Worth Scribbling in the Manuscript

---

# PART XIII — FINAL SYNTHESIS CHAPTER

Do NOT end with a summary.

Create a synthesis chapter.

Answer:

- Why does this paper matter?
- What are its deepest contributions?
- What ideas transfer?
- What assumptions deserve skepticism?
- What should a researcher remember years later?

The synthesis chapter should often be among the strongest chapters.

---

# PART XIV — APPENDIX REQUIREMENT

Create at least one cross-cutting appendix.

Purpose:

Collect a major mechanism distributed throughout the manuscript.

Examples:

- Amplification Mechanisms
- Network Effects
- Reputation Dynamics
- Success Mechanisms
- Hidden Assumptions
- Causal Architecture

The appendix should reveal the manuscript's deep structure.

---

# PART XV — QUALITY CONTROL CHECKLIST

Before finalizing a chapter verify:

Anchor Check
✓ Exact manuscript opening text

Quotation Check
✓ Important quotations preserved

Color Check
✓ BLACK BOLD quotations
✓ BLUE interpretation
✓ RED implications

Density Check
✓ Minimal wasted space

Mechanism Check
✓ Causal mechanisms identified

Transfer Check
✓ Ideas transferable beyond domain

Underline Check
✓ Quotations worth marking

Printing Check
✓ Suitable for annotation

---

# SUCCESS CRITERION

The finished product should feel like:

'Reading the paper beside an exceptionally insightful co-author.'

It should NOT feel like:

- Cliff Notes
- SparkNotes
- Executive summary
- Literature review

If it feels like a guided intellectual conversation with the manuscript, the task has succeeded.
